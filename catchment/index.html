    How to play

    Kids live along the streets. Open schools at the corners to reach as many kids as you can.

    • Kids. Each dot is the kids on one street, in hundreds. A 4 is about 400 kids.
    • School. Opens on a corner and reaches the 3 streets that meet there.
    • Magnet. Put it on a school. Kids come from one corner further: 6 more streets.
    • Safe Passage. Kids walk through this corner for free, so a school's reach carries on through it. It also gets kids past roadwork.
    • Roadwork. No one walks through, and no school can open here.
    • Closed. A real school that closed in 2013. Kids can walk past, but nothing new opens here.
    • Expressways and rivers. A red bar means there's no bridge or overpass nearby, so kids on that street can't walk to that corner.
    • Parks. Kids cut through big parks for free, so a school's reach carries on through a park corner.
    • Safe Passage routes. Real CPS routes with adults posted along them. Kids walk through these corners for free.
    • L stations. Kids can ride the L one stop, then walk as usual. A school at a station also reaches the streets around the next stop each way.

    Kids reached by two schools only count once. You get 3 checks. Each tells you how close you are to the best possible district. Reach 100% to win.

    A new board every day, cut from a real Chicago neighborhood. Kid counts come from the 2020 Census: children aged 5 to 14, spread onto a grid of streets about half a mile long. Catchment is part of The Student Shuffle, a game about where cities put their schools, first shown at the Chicago Architecture Biennial.
