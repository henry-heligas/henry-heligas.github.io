    How to play

    Kids live along the streets. Open schools at the corners to reach as many kids as you can.

    • Kids. Each dot is the kids on one street, in hundreds. A 4 is about 400 kids.
    • School. Opens on a corner and reaches the 3 streets that meet there.
    • Magnet. Put it on a school. Kids come from one corner further: 6 more streets.
    • Safe Passage. Kids walk through this corner for free, so a school's reach carries on through it. It also gets kids past roadwork.
    • Roadwork. No one walks through, and no school can open here.
    • Closed. A real school that closed in 2013. Kids can walk past, but nothing new opens here.
    • Expressways and rivers. A red bar means there's no bridge or overpass nearby, so kids on that street can't walk to that corner.
    • Parks. Kids cut through big parks for free, so a school's reach carries on through a park corner.
    • Safe Passage routes. Real CPS routes with adults posted along them. Kids walk through these corners for free.
    • L stations. Kids can ride the L one stop, then walk as usual. A school at a station also reaches the streets around the next stop each way.

    Kids reached by two schools only count once. You get 3 checks. Each tells you how close you are to the best possible district. Reach 100% to win.

    About
    
  
    Catchment
    
  
    A game of wits, strategy, and foresight. Every day you get one real Chicago neighborhood, a handful of schools, and three checks to find the district that reaches the most kids. Step into the world of urban planning and education, and prove that you have what it takes to lead your district to victory!
    

  
    The Student Shuffle
    
  
    Catchment is the one-player edition of The Student Shuffle, a board game about Chicago's school districts, built for the 2023 Chicago Architecture Biennial and installed in the James R. Thompson Center. Up to six teams placed Schools, Magnets, Safe Passages, Hazards and School Closures on a hex map of the city tiled with numbered student tokens. Kids in reach of two schools had Options, and their points were split. Competitive games ended by Classical, Showdown or Blitz rules. The youngest player went first.
    
  
    The board was destroyed in the building's renovation. The map and the kids are rebuilt here, one neighborhood a day.
    

  
    Gamifying the real
    
  
    The city is an imaginary imposition of order upon the territory of the real. In the summer of 1830, JAMES THOMPSON did exactly that to the ground where this game's board would stand 193 years later. He drew the first survey map of Chicago: a grid of blocks and lots laid over Potawatomi homeland, so that it could be sold to settlers and speculators heading west. Three years later, the Treaty of Chicago signed away the last of that land, and its people were pushed west of the Mississippi. The United States had invented a game with rules stacked in its favor, and filed the violence as paperwork. Once the grid was built, it became hard to see that the land had ever existed otherwise.
    
  
    Native people are still here. Chicago has one of the largest urban Native communities in the country, much of it the result of a 1950s federal program that moved families off reservations and into cities. And for generations, the front line of assimilation was children: federal boarding schools took them from their families to teach them English and make them forget the rest.
    
  
    Now YOU sit at the surveyor's table! Lay down the lines! Open the schools! Close a few! Watch real Chicago kids, counted by the U.S. Census Bureau, march off to the corners you pick! Can you outfox the Dan Ryan? Can you find the best district in a single check? A brand-new neighborhood every day! Fun for the whole family! Ages 8 to adult. No batteries required.
    

  
    School archipelago
    
  
    On May 22, 2013, the Chicago Board of Education voted to close 50 schools at once, the largest mass school closing in American history. Forty-nine were elementary schools, most of them on the South and West Sides. Their students were shuffled to receiving schools, often farther away, and the city stretched Safe Passage, adults in bright vests posted on corners along the walk to school, to cover the new routes.
    
  
    Both are on the board. A crossed-out school marks a corner where one closed in 2013, and nothing new may open there. Safe Passage comes as a piece you place, and as the real routes from the 2023–24 school year.
    

  
    Setup
    
  
    Catchment, it should be said, also lays a grid over Chicago.
    
  
  
      
    
    1. Tile the city with hexagons, centers exactly one mile apart. Each side is a street, about 0.58 mi long. Each place three streets meet is a corner. Chicago comes to 1,093 streets and 765 corners.
      2. 
    
    2. Place the kids. Take the children aged 5 to 14 in every census tract (American Community Survey, 2017–2021). Estimate each hexagon's count from the tracts it overlaps, deal it out evenly to its six sides, and round each street to the nearest hundred: 301,800 kids. They live at the exact middle of their street, 0.29 mi from the corner at either end.
      3. 
    
    3. Add the hazards, real and imaginary. Real: a walk is cut wherever it crosses an expressway or river with no bridge or overpass within 700 ft. Imaginary: roadwork, scattered at random.
      4. 
    
    4. Add the shortcuts. Each CTA station moves to its nearest corner, and kids can ride one stop down the real line. Kids cut through parks of 10 acres or more, and through corners with at least 900 ft of real Safe Passage route nearby.
      5. 
    
    5. Close the schools. The 45 CPS elementary schools on the 2012–13 school list and gone from 2013–14 go on their nearest corner, if one is within about a quarter mile.
      6. 
    
    6. Cut out a neighborhood where kids live. Monday's board is the smallest. By Sunday it's bigger, with more pieces and every feature switched on.
      7. 
    
    7. Solve it. Every board is solved before it comes out, by a branch-and-bound search that rules out every placement that can't win, checked against brute force on small boards. 100% is the true maximum. Nobody beats it, the computer included.
      8. 
    
    8. Finish, then compare your district with the CPS elementary schools that are really there, from the 2023 school map made for The Student Shuffle.
      9. 
  
    

  
    Not included
    
  
    Buses. Budgets. Teachers. Lunch. Building capacity. Attendance boundaries. Charter schools, selective enrollment, magnet lotteries and the rest of school choice. The Board of Education. Kids who don't live at the exact middle of a street. Streets that aren't hexagons.
